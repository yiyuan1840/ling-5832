How Many Words Do I Know in My Native Mandarin?

LING 5832 — Assignment 1, Part 1  •  Yiyuan Jia  •  August 31, 2026

1  The short answer

My native language is Mandarin Chinese. I know approximately 60,800 of the 70,414 headword entries of 《现代汉语词典》 (Contemporary Chinese Dictionary, 7th ed.; XHC7) — about 86% of the standard reference dictionary of Modern Standard Chinese — with a 95% confidence interval of 58,400–63,200, estimated by stratified random sampling of the dictionary’s full text and a two-stage self-test in which every claim of knowledge was checked against the revealed definition. That number hides three definitional choices — what a word is (unusually fraught in Mandarin), what knowing means, and what population “how many” ranges over — under which my defensible answers run from about 6,700 to 65,600. This report makes the choices explicit.

2  What counts as a “word”? Mandarin morphology and the unit problem

Mandarin makes “word” hard to pin down. The orthography marks no word boundaries (hence the word-segmentation step in Chinese NLP), and the salient folk unit is the 字 zì (character), roughly a morpheme-syllable; the linguistic word 词 cí spans one to four characters, and native intuitions about its boundaries are famously unstable (Packard 2000). Morphologically, Mandarin is strongly isolating — no agreement, case, or tense; only aspect clitics (了, 着, 过) and the restricted plural 们 — so the lemma-versus-wordform choice that can double an English count, and worse for Finnish, barely arises. The action is instead in word formation: the lexicon is built overwhelmingly by compounding (词典 ‘word-dictionary’ = dictionary), plus derivation and reduplication, so “how many words do you know” is partly “how productive is your compounding” — a question with no finite answer.

I operationalized word as headword entry in XHC7 (written form + pronunciation + sense set; 70,414 entries). This is countable and reproducible but inherits lexicographic compromises: 16% of entries are single-character 字头, many of them bound morphemes rather than free words (the ones I missed were mostly archaic literary characters like 坰 ‘open country’); 6,497 four-character entries are mostly 成语, structurally phrases; separable 离合词 like 睡觉 blur the word–phrase line; 2,334 forms map to multiple entries (啊 has five); and transparent compounds are simply absent — XHC7 lists 鸭蛋 ‘duck egg’ but not 鸡蛋 ‘hen’s egg,’ 牙刷 ‘toothbrush’ but not 电动牙刷 ‘electric toothbrush’ — though I know each unlisted member exactly as well as the listed one.

3  What counts as “knowing” a word?

“Know” is a two-stage judgment here, with the bare written form as stimulus (no pinyin, no gloss). Stage 1, self-rating: 不认识 / 眼熟 (form familiar, no meaning) / 认识 (can read it and give the approximate meaning) / 掌握 (could use it correctly myself). Stage 2: every claim of 认识 or 掌握 was checked against the revealed dictionary definition — 对 / 部分对 / 错了. Verification guards against self-report inflation; it caught me 25 times in 336 claims (7.4%) — including, embarrassingly for this course, 黏着语 ‘agglutinative language’ — with partial errors concentrated in semi-transparent 成语 (集腋成裘), and it also catches claiming a homographic form while thinking of a different entry. I score four operationalizations: receptive-strict (认识+, verified 对; the headline), receptive-lenient (对/部分对), productive (掌握 and 对), and self-report (认识+, unverified). Response times corroborate the scale: median 674 ms for 掌握 judgments versus 2.3–3.6 s for all other categories — the signature of automatic lexical access.

4  “How many”: estimation, not enumeration

No one can enumerate their lexicon, so “how many” here means a point estimate, with quantified sampling uncertainty, of a defined population quantity: the number of XHC7 entries I know (excluding front matter and the Western-alphabet appendix). I stratified the entries by headword length and drew a seeded random sample of 360 — 60/180/45/55/20 from the 1/2/3/4/5+-character strata (Nh = 11,424 / 45,086 / 6,690 / 6,497 / 717); long strata are deliberately oversampled and the weighting corrects for it. The estimator is the stratified total Σ Nh p̂h with finite-population-corrected standard errors, plus a conservative interval from per-stratum Wilson bounds since several p̂ sit near 1.

6  Where the method and the definitions come apart

  1. The dictionary is not my lexicon, in both directions. XHC7 (2016) omits my technical vocabulary, neologisms, and the transparent compounds I produce freely (undercount); it counts bound 字头 and splits homographs (overcount of “words”). Read the estimate as coverage of a curated reference lexicon — which also makes cross-language comparison treacherous: 17,000 English word families (Goulden, Nation & Read 1990) is a different unit over a different universe.
  2. “Productive” was never tested by production. 掌握 is still self-assessment; an actual production task would likely lower the 59,581.
  3. Verification is imperfect. 对 requires only one sense of a polysemous entry (entry-level, not sense-level, knowledge), and ≈6% of entries in the community transcription lack definitions — 25 of my 336 claims hit blanks, degenerating to self-report there.
  4. One subject, one session. The CIs quantify sampling error only, not the stability of my own judgments.

7  Conclusion

About 61,000 dictionary entries — six of every seven in the standard dictionary — under a receptive, definition-checked criterion. The more instructive answer is the decomposition Mandarin morphology forces: ≈6,700 of 11,424 single-character morpheme entries, essentially all mid-frequency compound vocabulary, nine in ten 成语, and beyond that an open set of transparent compounds no dictionary will list, because the morphology builds them on demand. “How many words do you know” has a numeric answer only after you decide what a word is; in Mandarin, that decision is most of the question.

8  Postscript: a public replication site

The instrument is deployed as a public web app at yiyuan1840.github.io/ling-5832/vocab-test/: five fixed 360-item forms (A–E; form A is the one reported here) drawn disjointly under the same stratified design with fixed seeds, with testers randomly assigned to a form. On completion the page computes the full stratified analysis client-side (the JavaScript estimator reproduces the Python reference values on my data exactly) and renders a printable per-tester report; data stay in the tester’s browser unless exported as JSON, which the analysis script accepts directly. Jointly the forms probe 1,800 distinct entries; within a form, testers are directly comparable, and across forms sampling error averages out.

Reproducibility: parsing, seeded sampling, test app, site source, analysis scripts, and my 360 raw judgments: github.com/yiyuan1840/ling-5832; dictionary text: the community transcription github.com/CNMan/XDHYCD7th (as a submodule).
