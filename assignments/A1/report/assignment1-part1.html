How Many Words Do I Know in My Native Mandarin?

LING 5832, Assignment 1, Part 1  •  Yiyuan Jia  •  August 31, 2026

1  The short answer

My native language is Mandarin Chinese. I know approximately 57,800 of the 70,414 headword entries of 《现代汉语词典》 (Contemporary Chinese Dictionary, 7th ed.; XHC7), about 82% of the standard reference dictionary of Modern Standard Chinese, with a 95% confidence interval of 55,000 to 60,500. The estimate comes from stratified random sampling of the dictionary and a self test in two stages that checked every claim against the revealed definition. That number hides three definitional choices: what a word is (unusually fraught in Mandarin), what knowing means, and what population “how many” ranges over. Under different choices my defensible answers run from about 5,500 to 64,700. This report makes the choices explicit.

2  What counts as a “word”? Mandarin morphology and the unit problem

Mandarin makes “word” hard to pin down. The orthography marks no word boundaries (hence the word segmentation step in Chinese NLP), and the salient folk unit is the 字 zì (character), roughly one morpheme per syllable; the linguistic word 词 cí spans one to four characters, and native intuitions about its boundaries are famously unstable (Packard 2000). Morphologically, Mandarin is strongly isolating: no agreement, case, or tense; only aspect clitics (了, 着, 过) and the restricted plural 们. The choice between lemma and wordform, which can double an English count, therefore barely arises. The action is instead in word formation: the lexicon is built overwhelmingly by compounding (词典, literally word plus dictionary, means dictionary), with some derivation and reduplication, so “how many words do you know” is partly “how productive is your compounding,” a question with no finite answer.

I operationalized word as headword entry in XHC7 (written form, pronunciation, and sense set; 70,414 entries). This is countable and reproducible but inherits lexicographic compromises: 16% of entries are single character 字头, many of them bound morphemes rather than free words (the ones I missed were mostly archaic literary characters like 谫 ‘shallow’); 6,497 four character entries are mostly 成语, structurally phrases; separable verb object items like 睡觉 blur the line between word and phrase; 2,334 forms map to multiple entries (啊 has five); and transparent compounds are simply absent. XHC7 lists 鸭蛋 ‘duck egg’ but not 鸡蛋 ‘hen’s egg,’ 牙刷 ‘toothbrush’ but not 电动牙刷 ‘electric toothbrush,’ though I know each unlisted member exactly as well as the listed one.

3  What counts as “knowing” a word?

“Know” is a judgment in two stages here, with the bare written form as stimulus (no pinyin, no gloss). Stage 1, a rating of myself: 不认识 / 眼熟 (form familiar, no meaning) / 认识 (can read it and give the approximate meaning) / 掌握 (could use it correctly myself). Stage 2: every claim of 认识 or 掌握 was checked against the revealed dictionary definition as 对 / 部分对 / 错了. Verification guards against the inflation of self report, and it caught me 34 times in 331 claims (10.3%): 18 outright wrong, among them the semi transparent idiom 狮子搏兔 and the old transliteration 哀的美敦书 ‘ultimatum,’ plus 16 partially right (韵白, 鞭辟近里). It also catches claims made while thinking of a different homographic entry. I score four operationalizations: strict receptive (认识 or above, verified 对; the headline), lenient receptive (对 or 部分对), productive (掌握 and 对), and self report (认识 or above, unverified). Response times corroborate the scale: median 653 ms for 掌握 judgments versus 1.8 to 2.8 seconds for all other categories, the signature of automatic lexical access.

4  “How many”: estimation, not enumeration

No one can enumerate their lexicon, so “how many” here means a point estimate, with quantified sampling uncertainty, of a defined population quantity: the number of XHC7 entries I know (excluding front matter and the appendix of Western alphabet items). I stratified the entries by headword length and drew seeded random samples of 360 items each: 60, 180, 45, 55, and 20 from the strata of one, two, three, four, and five or more characters (Nh = 11,424 / 45,086 / 6,690 / 6,497 / 717); long strata are deliberately oversampled and the weighting corrects for it. I took the test twice on disjoint samples, form A then form C of the public instrument of section 8; this report uses the newer form C run, with form A as a stability check. The estimator is the stratified total Σ Nh p̂h with standard errors under finite population correction, plus a conservative interval from Wilson bounds per stratum since several p̂ sit near 1.

Knowledge is high for multi character words (87% to 93% by stratum) but far lower (48%) for single character 字头, exactly where the dictionary stocks archaic, literary, and dialectal bound morphemes; multi character entries alone give 52,238 of 58,990 (89%). Verification removed about 6,900 entries from the self report estimate, and the scoring of 部分对 moves the answer by another 3,600. The stability check: form A, on disjoint items, gave a strict estimate of 60,844 with CI [58,441, 63,247]; the runs differ by about 3,100, or 1.7 standard errors of the difference, within what sampling error alone would produce, though my verification also grew stricter the second time (10.3% of claims caught versus 7.4%).

6  Where the method and the definitions come apart

  1. The dictionary is not my lexicon, in both directions. XHC7 (2016) omits my technical vocabulary, neologisms, and the transparent compounds I produce freely (an undercount); it counts bound 字头 and splits homographs (an overcount of “words”). Read the estimate as coverage of a curated reference lexicon, which also makes comparison across languages treacherous: 17,000 English word families (Goulden, Nation & Read 1990) is a different unit over a different universe.
  2. “Productive” was never tested by production. 掌握 is still self assessment; an actual production task would likely lower the 56,419.
  3. Verification is imperfect. 对 requires only one sense of a polysemous entry (knowledge at the level of entries, not senses), and about 6% of entries in the community transcription lack definitions; 18 of my 331 claims hit blanks, and verification degenerated to self report there.
  4. One subject, two sessions. The CIs quantify sampling error only, not the stability of my own judgments; the two administrations bound that instability at roughly the size of the sampling error itself.

7  Conclusion

About 58,000 dictionary entries, some 82% of the standard dictionary, under a strict receptive criterion with definition checking. The more instructive answer is the decomposition Mandarin morphology forces: roughly 5,500 of 11,424 single character morpheme entries, most of the mid frequency compound vocabulary, more than nine in ten 成语, and beyond that an open set of transparent compounds no dictionary will list, because the morphology builds them on demand. “How many words do you know” has a numeric answer only after you decide what a word is; in Mandarin, that decision is most of the question.

8  Postscript: a public replication site

The instrument is deployed as a public web app at yiyuan1840.github.io/ling-5832/vocab-test/: five fixed forms of 360 items (A through E), drawn disjointly under the same stratified design with fixed seeds, with each tester randomly assigned to one form. On completion the page computes the full stratified analysis in the browser (the JavaScript estimator reproduces the Python reference values exactly) and renders a printable personal report; data stay in the tester’s browser unless exported as JSON, which the analysis script accepts directly. Jointly the forms probe 1,800 distinct entries; within a form testers are directly comparable, and across forms sampling error averages out.

Reproducibility: parsing, seeded sampling, test app, site source, analysis scripts, and my raw judgments: github.com/yiyuan1840/ling-5832; dictionary text: the community transcription github.com/CNMan/XDHYCD7th (as a submodule).
