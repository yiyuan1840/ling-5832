## Assignment 1: How many words do you know?

An empirical estimate of my Mandarin vocabulary size by stratified random sampling of
《现代汉语词典》（第7版）(70,414 entries). See
[assignments/A1/assignment1-part1.pdf](assignments/A1/assignment1-part1.pdf) for the report.

**Take the test yourself** (≈25 min, in Chinese, generates your own statistical report):

> **https://yiyuan1840.github.io/ling-5832/vocab-test/**

### How it works

- 360 entries sampled from 5 length strata (seed 5832 — every tester gets the same sample, so results are comparable)
- Two-stage judgment: self-rating from the bare written form, then verification against the revealed dictionary definition (catches overclaiming)
- The report estimates entries known under four operationalizations of "know" (self-report / receptive-lenient / receptive-strict / productive), each as a stratified total Σ Nₕp̂ₕ with 95% CIs
- Everything runs client-side; your data stays in your browser unless you export it

## Assignment 2: Language models and author identification

A byte pair encoding tokenizer, bigram and trigram language models with add k smoothing,
perplexity, and an author identification classifier, trained on *The Hobbit* and *The Lost
World*. See [assignments/A2/assignment2.pdf](assignments/A2/assignment2.pdf) for the report.

On held out 50 word passages the classifier is correct on 255 of 255, and still reaches 97.6%
after removing the two easiest cues, the differing quote characters and the character names.

### What it found

- **Add 1 smoothing is a poor default.** Perplexity 678 at k = 1 against 234 at k = 0.01.
- **The trigram model loses to the bigram**, because it meets a context it has never seen 29.7%
  of the time against 0.2% for the bigram, and falls back to guessing.
- **Per token perplexity is not comparable across vocabulary sizes.** It appears to recommend
  the smallest vocabulary; the vocabulary was chosen on accuracy instead.
